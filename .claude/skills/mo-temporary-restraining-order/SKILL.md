# Missouri TRO and Preliminary Injunction

Assemble an emergency injunction request in Missouri circuit court that meets Rule 92's verification, notice, and bond requirements and argues the controlling four-factor test.

## When to use
- The user needs a court to stop or require conduct quickly (property being destroyed or sold, lockout, ongoing contract violation, interference with rights).
- The user is defending against a TRO or preliminary injunction and wants to oppose or dissolve it.
- Not for: domestic violence, stalking, or harassment protection - use `mo-orders-of-protection`; federal court injunctions - use `federal-tro-preliminary-injunction`.

## Gather first
- The specific act to be stopped (or compelled), who is doing it, and when the harm will occur.
- Why money damages later would be inadequate (unique property, loss of home, constitutional injury, ongoing harm).
- Whether and how the other side has been or can be notified; ability to post a bond.

## Workflow
1. Confirm there is an underlying claim. An injunction is a remedy, not a cause of action - plead a substantive count (e.g., breach of contract, trespass, declaratory judgment) in a verified petition plus a count or motion for injunctive relief.
2. Verify the facts. A TRO without notice requires specific facts shown by affidavit or verified petition that immediate and irreparable injury will result before the adverse party can be heard, plus a written certification of efforts made to give notice and why notice should not be required (Rule 92.02). Draft the verification under oath.
3. Argue the four factors adopted in State ex rel. Director of Revenue v. Gabbert, 925 S.W.2d 838 (Mo. banc 1996): (a) probability of success on the merits; (b) threat of irreparable harm absent the injunction; (c) balance between that harm and the injury the injunction would inflict on other parties; (d) the public interest. Address each with facts, not conclusions.
4. Define the relief narrowly. The order must state reasons, be specific in terms, and describe in reasonable detail the acts restrained (Rule 92.02 - read the form-of-order text). Draft a proposed order that a sheriff could enforce.
5. Address the bond. Rule 92.02 conditions a TRO or preliminary injunction on the applicant giving a bond in an amount the court deems proper to cover costs and damages if the party is wrongfully enjoined. Propose a realistic amount and explain why; if indigent, ask the court to set a nominal bond and explain inability to pay.
6. Understand duration. A TRO issued without notice is short-lived (Rule 92.02 caps the initial period and limits extensions - verify the exact days) and the preliminary injunction hearing is set promptly. A preliminary injunction requires notice and a hearing.
7. Prepare for the hearing: witnesses, exhibits, photographs, a timeline, and a proposed order. Bring proof of notice/service.
8. Opposing side: attack likelihood of success, show an adequate remedy at law, highlight harm to the defendant and public, seek a substantial bond, and move to dissolve or modify. Damages for wrongful injunction are recoverable against the bond.
9. Appellate posture: some injunction orders are immediately appealable under RSMo 512.020 - verify which (granting, dissolving, refusing) before deciding to appeal or wait.

## Output
- Verified Petition for TRO and Preliminary Injunction outline: Caption; Parties; Jurisdiction/Venue; Facts; Count(s) on the merits; Count for Injunctive Relief (Gabbert factors); Prayer; Verification.
- Rule 92 certification of notice efforts.
- Proposed Order: findings on each factor, specific acts restrained, duration, bond amount, hearing date.
- Hearing checklist (witnesses, exhibits, proof of service).

## Pitfalls
- Unverified petition or conclusory affidavit ("I will be harmed") - TRO denied.
- No certification of notice efforts for an ex parte request.
- Seeking an order that is vague or broader than the threatened harm.
- Ignoring the bond requirement or proposing no amount.
- Asking for money damages as the "irreparable" harm - economic loss compensable later usually is not irreparable.
- Letting the TRO expire without the preliminary injunction hearing being set.
- Confusing a civil TRO with an order of protection under RSMo ch. 455.
- Delay: waiting weeks after learning of the harm undercuts any claim of urgency or irreparable injury.
- Seeking a mandatory injunction (ordering affirmative action) without acknowledging courts scrutinize those more closely.
- Failing to serve the TRO and petition promptly on the restrained party - an unserved order is hard to enforce.

## Verify before relying
- Pull verbatim Rule 92.02 and RSMo ch. 526 (and 512.020 for appealability) via `statute-lookup`.
- Confirm Gabbert and any later case you cite remain good law on CourtListener.
- Recompute TRO expiration and hearing dates from the actual issuance time.
