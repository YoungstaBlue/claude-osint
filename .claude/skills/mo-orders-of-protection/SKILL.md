# Missouri Orders of Protection

Prepares a petitioner or respondent for a Missouri order-of-protection case: who qualifies, what must be proved, how fast it moves, and what a full order does.

## When to use
- Seeking protection from domestic violence, stalking, or sexual assault, for an adult or a child
- Served with an ex parte order and a hearing date as respondent
- Renewing, modifying, or ending an existing order; assessing collateral consequences (firearms, custody)
- Not for: civil injunctions between non-qualifying parties (use `mo-temporary-restraining-order`); custody litigation itself (use `mo-family-law-dissolution-custody`)

## Gather first
- The relationship between the parties (family or household member, dating, none) and every incident with date, place, what happened, witnesses
- Evidence: texts, call logs, social media, photos, medical records, police reports, prior orders
- Any pending dissolution, custody, or criminal case involving the same parties

## Workflow
1. **Eligibility (RSMo 455.020).** A person subjected to domestic violence by a present or former family or household member, or who has been the victim of stalking or sexual assault (by anyone), may petition. Child orders: a verified petition for a child subjected to domestic violence by a present or former household member, or to stalking or sexual assault by any person (RSMo 455.505).
2. **Definitions (RSMo 455.010).** "Abuse" includes listed acts, attempts, or threats (assault, battery, coercion, harassment, sexual assault, unlawful imprisonment, among others); "domestic violence" is abuse or stalking committed by a family or household member; stalking and harassment have course-of-conduct and alarm/distress elements. Quote the current definitions verbatim; the list has been amended repeatedly.
3. **Petition.** Verified petition on the court's form, specific incidents with dates, the relief requested. No filing fees, court costs, or bond may be assessed to the petitioner (RSMo 455.027).
4. **Ex parte order (RSMo 455.035).** May issue immediately on a verified petition showing good cause; an immediate and present danger of domestic violence constitutes good cause. Respondent must be served for the full hearing.
5. **Full hearing (RSMo 455.040).** Held within 15 days after filing unless continued for good cause. Petitioner must prove domestic violence, stalking, or sexual assault by a preponderance of the evidence, and the respondent may show the conduct was otherwise justified under the law.
6. **Duration and renewal (RSMo 455.040).** A full order lasts at least 180 days and not more than one year, unless after an evidentiary hearing the court makes specific written findings that the respondent poses a serious danger to the physical or mental health of the petitioner or a minor household member - then at least two years and not more than ten years. Renewal is on motion and hearing before expiration, with the same duration structure. Calendar the expiration date and file any renewal motion well before it.
7. **Relief available (RSMo 455.050).** Restraining contact and abuse, excluding respondent from the residence, temporary custody and visitation, support, and other listed relief; read the current section for each remedy requested.
8. **Respondent's defense.** Attend the hearing; request a continuance if not given adequate time; cross-examine on dates, specifics, and inconsistencies; offer documentary evidence (texts, location data) and witnesses; object to hearsay. Consider the Fifth Amendment if a parallel criminal case exists - anything said can be used there. Consent orders still carry consequences.
9. **Consequences.** Violating an ex parte or full order is a crime (RSMo 455.085 - verify current class and enhancement). Federal law prohibits firearm possession by a person subject to a qualifying order issued after notice and hearing protecting an intimate partner or child (18 U.S.C. 922(g)(8)); upheld in United States v. Rahimi, 602 U.S. 680 (2024). Orders can affect custody determinations under RSMo 452.375.
10. **Modification and appeal.** The court may modify an order after notice and hearing on a motion with an affidavit showing changed circumstances (RSMo 455.060). A full order is appealable; post-judgment and appeal timing follow the civil rules - see `mo-appeals-procedure`.

## Output
- Incident chart: | Date | Incident | Category (abuse type / stalking / sexual assault) | Evidence | Witness |
- Hearing outline for the side being represented: elements, exhibits in order, witness questions, anticipated defenses or arguments
- Key dates: filing, ex parte service, hearing (filing + 15 days), order expiration, renewal-motion target date

## Pitfalls
- Vague petitions ("he harasses me") without dated specific incidents.
- Respondent not appearing - a full order is usually entered by default.
- Contact initiated by the petitioner does not license the respondent to violate the order.
- Missing the renewal before expiration; a lapsed order generally requires a new petition.
- Ignoring the firearms consequence and custody effects when consenting to an order.
- Exaggerating or adding unverifiable allegations; credibility decides most full hearings.
- Respondents contacting the petitioner to "work it out" after service - that contact can itself violate the ex parte order.
- Failing to bring the evidence in printable, authenticated form (screenshots with dates, phone visible).
- Assuming an order of protection settles custody permanently; it is temporary relief tied to the order.
