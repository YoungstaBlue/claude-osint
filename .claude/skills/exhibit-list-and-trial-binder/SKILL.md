# Exhibit List and Trial Binder

Builds the exhibit list, witness list, stipulation proposals, and a trial binder so every exhibit can be found,
offered, and admitted without delay.

## When to use
- A trial, evidentiary hearing, or bench trial is set
- The court's scheduling or pretrial order requires exchange of exhibit and witness lists
- Proposing stipulations to admissibility or authenticity
- Not for: whether an exhibit is admissible (use `authentication-foundation-builder`, `hearsay-analyzer`)

## Gather first
- The scheduling/pretrial order and local rules (they control numbering, deadlines, and format)
- Trial or hearing date and judge's standing orders or courtroom procedures
- The evidence set, ideally already in a source-cited timeline

## Workflow
1. Read the order. Missouri circuit courts set exhibit/witness requirements by pretrial order and local circuit
   rule; federal courts use FRCP 16 orders and local rules. In federal court, absent a different order, FRCP
   26(a)(3) pretrial disclosures (witnesses, deposition designations, exhibits) are due at least 30 days before
   trial, and objections within 14 days after; unlisted objections (other than FRE 402/403) may be waived.
2. Number exhibits using the court's convention (commonly plaintiff numbers, defendant letters, or a party prefix
   with numbers). Number in the order you will use them or by category; never renumber after exchange.
3. Build the exhibit list with columns the court expects, plus internal working columns.
4. Pre-mark: exhibit sticker on every copy (original for the clerk/witness, judge's copy, opposing party's copy,
   your copy). Bates-stamp multi-page exhibits.
5. Propose stipulations: send opposing party a list proposing stipulation to authenticity and/or admissibility
   for routine items (contracts, correspondence, public records). Get agreements in writing or on the record.
6. Prepare demonstratives separately (charts, timelines, summaries). Voluminous-record summaries need
   FRE 1006 compliance in federal court (underlying records made available).
7. Assemble the trial binder (tabs):
   1. Case summary and theory (one page)
   2. Pleadings and key orders (pretrial order, rulings in limine)
   3. Elements chart and proposed jury instructions or findings
   4. Witness list with outlines (direct/cross) and order of call
   5. Exhibit list, then exhibits in number order
   6. Deposition excerpts and prior statements for impeachment (page/line)
   7. Motions in limine and evidence briefs (hearsay, foundation responses)
   8. Opening and closing outlines
   9. Blank exhibit tracking sheet and law-on-call cards (key rules with citations)
8. During trial, track each exhibit: offered, objection, ruling, admitted or refused. Re-offer refused exhibits
   with an offer of proof if needed.

## Output
- Exhibit list:
| Ex. No. | Description | Bates/pages | Sponsoring witness | Foundation route | Stipulated? | Offered | Objection | Ruling |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
- Witness list: Name / Contact / Subject of testimony / Will call or may call / Subpoena served? / Est. time
- Stipulation letter draft listing exhibits proposed for stipulation
- Binder tab index (as above)

## Pitfalls
- Missing the exchange deadline; courts exclude unlisted exhibits and witnesses.
- Failing to subpoena witnesses; a listed witness who does not appear is not the court's problem.
- Renumbering exhibits after exchange, creating a confused record on appeal.
- Not tracking rulings; an exhibit never formally admitted is not evidence.
- Unredacted personal identifiers in exhibits filed or shown publicly.

## Verify before relying
- Read the actual scheduling order, local rules, and judge's procedures; they override general practice.
- Pull current text of FRCP 16 and 26(a)(3) and any Missouri rule or local rule cited via `statute-lookup`.
- Recompute exchange and objection deadlines from the trial setting.
- Legal information, not legal advice.
