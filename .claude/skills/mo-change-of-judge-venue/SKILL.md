# Missouri Change of Judge and Change of Venue

Uses Missouri's strict, short windows for an automatic change of judge or venue, and builds a factual record for for-cause requests when the window has closed.

## When to use
- A civil case was just filed or a judge was just assigned and you want a different judge
- You believe the judge is biased or has a conflict
- The case is in a small county and you want it tried elsewhere, or venue is improper
- Not for: criminal cases, which have separate change-of-judge and venue rules in Rule 32 (verify the specific rule); federal recusal (28 U.S.C. 144 and 455)

## Gather first
- Date you were served with process and date the current trial judge was designated (and any notice of designation)
- Whether you or another party on your side already used a change of judge or venue
- County population (for Rule 51.03) and the specific facts showing bias or conflict, if any

## Workflow
1. **Change of judge without cause (Rule 51.05 - verify).** Each side gets one change of judge as of right by filing an application; no reasons are required. Timing: within 60 days from service of process or 30 days from designation of the trial judge, whichever time is longer (verify). If the judge was designated within the last 30 days of trial or the timing rules differ for your case type, check the rule's special provisions. Serve all parties. The judge must sustain a timely, proper application.
2. **Associate circuit and special proceedings.** Confirm whether Rule 51.05 or a statute governs the change in associate circuit, small claims, probate, or other special proceedings (verify; some statutes impose shorter windows).
3. **Change of judge for cause (Rule 51.07 - verify).** Available when the no-cause window has passed or was used. Requires a showing of actual bias, prejudice, interest, or another disqualifying ground. The Code of Judicial Conduct, Rule 2-2.11, requires disqualification where the judge's impartiality might reasonably be questioned, with listed examples (personal bias, financial interest, prior involvement, relationship to a party or lawyer). Adverse rulings alone are almost never sufficient. Use a verified application or affidavit stating specific facts; request a hearing before a different judge if the rule provides for it.
4. **Change of venue as of right (Rule 51.03 - verify).** In civil actions triable by jury pending in a county with 75,000 or fewer inhabitants, a party may obtain a change of venue on timely application, with timing similar to Rule 51.05 (verify exact period). Confirm the current population of the county from the latest census.
5. **Change of venue for cause (Rule 51.04 - verify).** Grounds include prejudice of the inhabitants or undue influence of the adverse party over the inhabitants; requires a verified application and supporting affidavits per the rule, and the opposing party may deny.
6. **Improper venue (Rule 51.045 - verify).** If the case was filed in a county where venue is improper under RSMo 508.010, file a motion to transfer within the time stated (generally 60 days after service). The opponent's failure to reply may result in transfer; this is a transfer, not a dismissal. Also preserve the venue objection under Rule 55.27 (see `mo-motion-to-dismiss`).
7. **Draft the application.** Caption; title ("Application for Change of Judge" / "Application for Change of Venue"); cite the rule; state the dates establishing timeliness; for cause-based requests, numbered specific facts; verification if required; certificate of service.
8. **After ruling.** Track the new judge's assignment and reset your deadlines. A wrongly denied timely Rule 51.05 application is commonly reviewed by writ of prohibition or mandamus rather than waiting for appeal (see `writs-mandamus-prohibition`, verify).

## Output
- Timeliness table: | Request type | Trigger date | Window | Last day | Already used? |
- Draft application with caption, rule citation, timeliness facts, (for cause) numbered factual allegations, verification, certificate of service
- Decision note: as-of-right option available? for-cause facts sufficient? strategic cost of changing judge or venue

## Pitfalls
- Missing the 60/30-day window; afterward only for-cause changes remain and the bar is high.
- Asking for a change of judge because of adverse rulings.
- Accusatory or intemperate language in a for-cause application - it can damage credibility with any judge.
- Assuming the federal recusal standard or federal timing applies in state court.
- Using up the side's one no-cause change early when co-parties also wanted it - coordinate.
- Forgetting that some special proceedings have their own shorter change-of-judge statutes.
- Failing to serve the application on all parties, which can delay or defeat it.
- Not checking whether a co-party's earlier application counts as your side's one change.
- Filing for-cause allegations based on rumor rather than sworn, firsthand facts.
- Treating an improper-venue transfer motion as optional after raising venue in an answer; follow the Rule 51.045 procedure (verify).

## Verify before relying
- Pull verbatim Rules 51.03, 51.04, 51.045, 51.05, 51.07 and Rule 2-2.11 via `statute-lookup` (or Descrybe `search_laws_and_rules`); confirm current windows and exceptions.
- Confirm county population with current census data for Rule 51.03.
- Check any case law on writ review or for-cause standards is still good law (CourtListener / Descrybe treatment).
- Recompute deadlines from actual service and designation dates.
