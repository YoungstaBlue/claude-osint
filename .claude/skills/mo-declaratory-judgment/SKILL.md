# Missouri Declaratory Judgment

Decide whether a dispute qualifies for declaratory relief in Missouri and draft a petition that survives a justiciability challenge.

## When to use
- The user wants a court to declare rights under a contract, deed, will, trust, insurance policy, statute, ordinance, or regulation.
- The user wants to challenge the validity or constitutionality of an ordinance or statute before (or instead of) being prosecuted or penalized.
- The opposing party moved to dismiss a declaratory count as non-justiciable or unripe.
- Not for: review of a contested agency decision - use `mo-administrative-appeals`; emergency stop-the-conduct relief - use `mo-temporary-restraining-order` (often paired).

## Gather first
- The document, statute, or ordinance in question (exact text) and the specific provision in dispute.
- The concrete facts showing the parties actually disagree now (letters, denials, threats of enforcement, refusals).
- Every person or entity whose interests the declaration would affect.

## Workflow
1. Authority: Rule 87 and the Missouri Declaratory Judgments Act (RSMo 527.010 to 527.130) authorize courts to declare rights, status, and other legal relations. Relief is discretionary.
2. Test justiciability. Missouri requires: (a) a justiciable controversy - a real, substantial, presently existing controversy admitting of specific relief, as distinguished from an advisory opinion; (b) a plaintiff with a legally protectable interest at stake; (c) a controversy ripe for determination; and (d) no adequate remedy at law. Missouri Soybean Ass'n v. Missouri Clean Water Comm'n, 102 S.W.3d 10 (Mo. banc 2003), discusses these requirements, especially ripeness in pre-enforcement challenges. Map each element to specific facts.
3. Ripeness check for pre-enforcement challenges: is the issue purely legal, is the law being applied or threatened against the plaintiff, and would delay cause hardship? Hypothetical future disputes fail.
4. Adequate remedy check: if a pending lawsuit, administrative procedure, or ordinary claim (e.g., breach of contract damages) fully resolves the issue, the court may decline. Explain why declaratory relief adds something.
5. Parties and notice. Join all persons who have or claim an interest affected by the declaration (Rule 87.04; RSMo 527.110). If a municipal ordinance's validity is at issue, the municipality must be a party; if a statute is challenged as unconstitutional, the attorney general must be served and is entitled to be heard. Confirm exact requirements in the verbatim text.
6. Draft the declaration sought precisely: "Plaintiff requests a declaration that [provision] does not require [X] / that [ordinance section] is void as applied because [Y]." Vague prayers invite dismissal.
7. Add supplemental relief where needed: RSMo ch. 527 allows further relief based on the declaration when necessary or proper; pair with injunctive or damages counts as separate counts.
8. Constitutional challenges: raise the constitutional issue at the earliest opportunity, cite the specific constitutional provision and section, and state facts showing the violation - Missouri preservation rules are strict. Note that cases involving the validity of a Missouri statute may fall within the Supreme Court of Missouri's exclusive appellate jurisdiction (Mo. Const. art. V, sec. 3 - verify).
9. Anticipate defenses: lack of standing, unripe, moot, adequate remedy at law, failure to join necessary parties, sovereign immunity (for damages, not usually for declaratory relief - verify).

## Output
- Justiciability matrix: Element | Facts supporting | Evidence | Weak spots.
- Petition outline: Caption; Parties (all interested persons); Jurisdiction/Venue; Facts; Count I - Declaratory Judgment (text of provision, competing interpretations, justiciable controversy allegations); additional counts; Prayer listing each declaration sought; Certificate of service on attorney general/municipality if required.
- Necessary-parties checklist.

## Pitfalls
- Asking for an advisory opinion about something that has not happened.
- Failing to join a necessary party or serve the attorney general in a statutory constitutional challenge.
- Raising a constitutional claim late or without citing the specific provision - it may be waived.
- Using declaratory judgment to relitigate an issue already decided or pending elsewhere.
- Pleading conclusions ("the ordinance is unconstitutional") instead of facts.
- Suing an official who has no role in enforcing the challenged law - the controversy may not be with that defendant.
- Seeking money damages only under a declaratory label - plead a separate count.
- Ignoring mootness: if the dispute resolves (contract expires, ordinance repealed), the claim may be dismissed unless an exception applies.
- Failing to attach or quote the exact contract, policy, or ordinance language at issue.
- Overlooking a jury issue: disputed fact questions in a declaratory action may be tried as in other civil actions - check Rule 87 text.

## Verify before relying
- Pull verbatim Rule 87, RSMo 527.010-527.130, and any constitutional provision via `statute-lookup`.
- Confirm Missouri Soybean and any other justiciability case remain good law on CourtListener before citing.
- Check whether an administrative exhaustion requirement applies first.
- Legal information, not legal advice; constitutional challenges are complex - consult counsel or legal aid.
