# Missouri Pro Se Courtroom Procedure

Prepare a self-represented litigant to conduct a Missouri hearing or trial professionally and leave a complete record for any appeal.

## When to use
- The user has an upcoming hearing, motion argument, or trial in a Missouri state court and is representing himself.
- The user asks how to object, preserve error, make an offer of proof, or get a transcript.
- Not for: trial planning and deadlines - use `mo-pretrial-and-trial-prep`; specific objection grounds - use `objection-playbook`.

## Gather first
- Court, division, judge, hearing type (motion hearing, evidentiary hearing, bench trial, jury trial), date and time.
- Whether the proceeding will be recorded or reported (ask the clerk) - essential for appeal.
- What the user must prove or oppose at the hearing and the exhibits/witnesses planned.

## Workflow
1. Standard to expect: Missouri courts hold pro se litigants to the same rules and procedures as attorneys; the judge cannot act as the litigant's lawyer. Plan accordingly.
2. Before the hearing: confirm the setting on Case.net, arrive early, bring three copies of every exhibit (court, opponent, witness) plus your own, a short outline, proposed order, and pen/paper. Dress in conservative business attire.
3. Make sure a record exists. Ask the clerk in advance whether the division records proceedings or uses a court reporter; if not, request one or ask how the proceeding will be recorded. Without a record, appellate review is largely impossible.
4. Courtroom conduct: stand when the judge enters and when speaking (unless told otherwise); address the judge as "Your Honor"; speak to the judge, not opposing party; do not interrupt; ask permission before approaching a witness or the bench; no recording devices unless authorized (Missouri Supreme Court Operating Rule 16 governs recording/broadcast - verify current text and local orders).
5. Presenting: give a 1-2 minute roadmap of what you are asking for and why; state the legal basis (rule/statute) and the key facts; then present evidence through witnesses and exhibits. Statements by the litigant at the podium are argument, not evidence, unless sworn as a witness.
6. Exhibits: have each exhibit marked, show it to opposing party, lay a foundation through a witness (or stipulation), then offer it ("I offer Exhibit 3 into evidence"). Get an express ruling.
7. Objections: object promptly and state the specific ground ("Objection, hearsay"). Get a ruling. If overruled on an important point, ask for a continuing objection to the same line of questioning.
8. Offer of proof: if the judge excludes your evidence, make an offer of proof on the record - state what the witness would say (or have the witness answer outside the jury's presence), why it is relevant, and the purpose. Without it, the exclusion usually cannot be reviewed on appeal.
9. Request rulings on the record and written orders; ask the court to state its ruling for the record if unclear. Request findings where available (bench trial - before evidence, Rule 73.01).
10. After: order the transcript if an appeal or post-trial motion is possible; calendar post-judgment deadlines immediately.

## Output
- Hearing script outline: Introduction (name, party, relief sought); Legal basis; Evidence plan (witness -> exhibit -> point proved); Responses to anticipated arguments; Specific relief and proposed order.
- Day-of checklist: copies, exhibit labels, proposed order, outline, ID, case number, record confirmation.
- Phrase card: objection phrasing, offer of proof phrasing, asking for continuing objection, asking for a ruling.

## Pitfalls
- No court reporter or recording - nothing to appeal.
- Arguing facts without evidence in the record.
- Objecting without a specific ground, or failing to obtain a ruling.
- No offer of proof after excluded evidence.
- Speaking directly to the opposing party or ex parte contact with the judge's chambers.
- Losing composure; interrupting the judge.
- Leaving without knowing what was ordered and when the next deadline is.
- Bringing new documents the other side has never seen to an evidentiary hearing without disclosure - exclusion risk.
- Assuming the judge read your filings - summarize the key point orally.
- Not asking for a written order or docket entry reflecting the ruling before leaving.

## Verify before relying
- Pull verbatim text of any rule cited (Rule 73.01; Operating Rule 16) via `statute-lookup`; check the circuit's local rules and the judge's standing orders.
- Confirm any case quoted on pro se standards on CourtListener before citing it in a filing.
- Recompute post-hearing deadlines from the docket entry date.
- Legal information, not legal advice; limited-scope counsel or legal aid can help with critical hearings.
