# Missouri Family Law: Dissolution and Custody

Maps a Missouri dissolution, custody, support, or modification case to its statutory requirements, deadlines, and the evidence the court must have to make each finding.

## When to use
- Filing or responding to a dissolution, legal separation, or paternity-related custody case
- Building a proposed parenting plan or a Form 14 child support calculation
- Modifying custody, visitation, support, or maintenance; responding to a relocation notice
- Not for: orders of protection (use `mo-orders-of-protection`); estate planning after divorce (use `mo-wills-trusts-probate`)

## Gather first
- The petition or existing judgment, date of service, and county where filed
- Income documents for both parties (pay stubs, tax returns), child-care, health insurance, and extraordinary child expenses
- A list of assets and debts with dates acquired and how titled; children's current schedule and school

## Workflow
1. **Jurisdiction and grounds (RSMo 452.305).** One party must have been a Missouri resident (or a service member stationed here) for 90 days before filing; 30 days must pass after filing before judgment; and the court must find the marriage irretrievably broken. If the other spouse denies that under oath, the court considers the statutory factors in RSMo 452.320 (verify list). Custody jurisdiction is governed by the UCCJEA (RSMo 452.700 et seq.), generally the child's home state.
2. **Pleadings (RSMo 452.310).** Petition must be verified. Respondent must file a verified answer within 30 days of service to avoid an interlocutory default. Both parties submit a proposed parenting plan within 30 days after service or entry of appearance where custody is at issue. Use the circuit's required forms (statements of property, income and expense) - check the local court rules.
3. **Custody (RSMo 452.375).** Best interests of the child, applying the statutory factors (including each parent's wishes and proposed plan, the child's need for frequent, continuing, and meaningful contact with both parents, each parent's willingness to perform parental functions and to allow contact, the child's adjustment, mental and physical health of all involved, any intended relocation, and the child's wishes). Missouri public policy favors frequent, continuing, meaningful contact with both parents. Domestic violence findings are required where abuse is shown. The court must make written findings when custody is contested or a plan is rejected (verify triggering conditions).
4. **Child support (RSMo 452.340; Rule 88.01).** Form 14 produces a rebuttable presumed amount; deviation requires a written or on-record finding that the amount is unjust or inappropriate. Inputs: gross incomes, imputed income for voluntarily underemployed parents, overnight-visitation credit, work-related child care, health insurance, extraordinary expenses. Support generally continues to 18, or longer if the child is enrolled in higher education per the statute's conditions (to age 21 limit - verify details in 452.340).
5. **Property and debt (RSMo 452.330).** Set apart each spouse's separate property, then divide marital property and debt in a just manner considering the statutory factors (economic circumstances, contributions including homemaking, value of separate property, conduct during marriage, custodial arrangements). Trace separate property; commingling can transmute it.
6. **Maintenance (RSMo 452.335).** Requesting spouse must lack sufficient property, including marital property apportioned, to meet reasonable needs and be unable to support themselves through appropriate employment (or be custodian of a child whose condition makes outside work inappropriate). Then the amount and duration factors apply. Decree must state whether modifiable.
7. **Modification.**
   - Support/maintenance: changed circumstances so substantial and continuing as to make the terms unreasonable; a Form 14 change of 20% or more from the existing amount is prima facie evidence of such change (RSMo 452.370).
   - Custody: facts arising since the prior decree or unknown at the time show a change in circumstances of the child or custodian, and modification serves the child's best interests (RSMo 452.410).
8. **Relocation (RSMo 452.377).** Relocation means a change of the child's principal residence for 90 days or more. Written notice by certified mail at least 60 days before the move with the statutory contents. The other parent may file a motion to prevent relocation, with a good-faith affidavit, within 30 days of receiving notice; otherwise the move may proceed after 60 days.

## Output
- Filing checklist with deadlines: residency date, filing date, 30-day waiting date, answer date, parenting-plan date
- Form 14 worksheet inputs table: | Line item | Parent A | Parent B | Source document |
- Property schedule: | Asset/debt | Marital or separate | Value | Evidence | Proposed allocation |
- Best-interest factor chart: | 452.375 factor | Facts | Evidence | Witness |
- Draft parenting plan outline (schedule, holidays, decision-making, transportation, communication, dispute resolution)

## Pitfalls
- Failing to file a verified answer and parenting plan within 30 days.
- Unsupported Form 14 inputs (no proof of child-care costs or the other parent's income).
- Moving with the child without proper 452.377 notice - it can be used against the relocating parent in custody.
- Mixing separate funds into joint accounts or property without records.
- Asking to modify support for a temporary or voluntary income drop.
- Agreeing to a settlement without running the Form 14 numbers or valuing retirement accounts.
- Assuming the 30-day waiting period means judgment will enter on day 31; contested cases take far longer.

## Verify before relying
- Pull verbatim text of RSMo 452.305, 452.310, 452.320, 452.330, 452.335, 452.340, 452.370, 452.375, 452.377, 452.410, Rule 88.01, and the current Form 14 and Directions via `statute-lookup` or Descrybe `search_laws_and_rules`. Several of these sections were amended recently; confirm current effective text.
- Check any custody or support case relied on for current treatment (CourtListener / Descrybe).
- Recompute all deadlines from the actual service or notice-receipt date.
- Legal information, not legal advice; custody outcomes are high-stakes - recommend counsel or legal aid.
