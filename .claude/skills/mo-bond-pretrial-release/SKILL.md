# Missouri Bond and Pretrial Release

Drafts the argument and motion to obtain release or reduce bond in a Missouri criminal case, framed around the controlling Rule 33.01 presumption of release on the least restrictive conditions.

## When to use
- Defendant is detained, cannot post bond, or faces burdensome conditions (GPS cost, curfew, no-contact terms)
- "Motion to reduce bond", "bond review", "release hearing", "held without bond", "conditions are excessive"
- Defending against a state motion to increase bond or revoke release
- Not for: municipal ordinance warrants (Rule 37.16-37.22; see `mo-municipal-ordinance-defense`)

## Gather first
- Current release order: amount, type (cash only, surety, 10 percent, recognizance), and every non-monetary condition
- Defendant's ties: residence length, family, employment/school, income and expenses (ability to pay), health, appearance history
- Charge, criminal history, whether on probation/parole/pretrial release at the time of the offense, and any risk-assessment score used

## Workflow
1. **Start from the rule's presumption.** Rule 33.01(a): a defendant charged with a bailable offense "shall be entitled to be released." Rule 33.01(c): release on own recognizance with only the standard conditions unless the court determines that will not secure appearance or the safety of the community or another person.
2. **Least restrictive conditions.** Rule 33.01(c) requires the least restrictive condition or combination; conditions greater than necessary are prohibited. The court must first consider non-monetary conditions; a monetary condition set "at more than is necessary" after considering ability to pay is "impermissible." Build the argument as: non-monetary conditions X, Y, Z adequately address any risk; therefore no money condition (or a smaller one) is permitted.
3. **Detention requires clear and convincing evidence.** Rule 33.01(d): detention only if the court finds by clear and convincing evidence that no combination of conditions will secure safety. A detained defendant may file a written request after arraignment for trial within 120 days (waived by defense continuance). Calendar that request.
4. **Apply the Rule 33.01(e) factors** individually: nature and circumstances of offense; weight of evidence; family ties, employment, financial resources including ability to pay, character, mental condition; residence length; convictions; appearance record; whether on probation/parole/release at the time; any approved validated risk-assessment tool. Parallel statutory factors: RSMo 544.455.
5. **Demand findings.** Rule 33.01(f): the court must make written or oral findings on the record supporting detention or conditions, and either party may make a record on financial status and ability to pay. Ask for findings explicitly.
6. **Timing.** If detained after the initial appearance, a release hearing must occur within 7 days excluding weekends and holidays, absent good cause (Rule 33.05). Later reviews: a new application under Rule 33.01 or a motion to modify under Rule 33.06(a)(2) (conditions excessive) or changed circumstances.
7. **Evidence.** Rule 33 proceedings are informal; rules of evidence need not apply (Rule 33.07). Bring letters from employers/family, pay stubs, lease, proof of treatment enrollment.
8. **Constitutional backstop.** Mo. Const. art. I, sec. 20 (bailable by sufficient sureties) and sec. 21 (no excessive bail); Eighth Amendment; Stack v. Boyle, 342 U.S. 1 (1951) (bail set higher than reasonably calculated to assure appearance is excessive).
9. **If denied.** Further review is by writ (habeas corpus or a writ in the appellate court), not ordinary appeal - see `writs-mandamus-prohibition`.

## Output
- **Motion to Modify Conditions of Release** with headings: Procedural History; Current Conditions; Rule 33.01 Standard; Factors Applied (one paragraph per 33.01(e) factor); Proposed Less Restrictive Conditions; Ability to Pay; Request for Findings Under Rule 33.01(f); Prayer
- A one-page financial declaration outline
- A hearing script: 2-minute oral argument plus exhibit list

## Pitfalls
- Arguing only "the bond is too high" without proposing specific alternative conditions the court can adopt.
- Not making a record on ability to pay - without it there is nothing to review.
- Discussing the facts of the offense at the hearing; anything said can be used at trial.
- Missing the victim's right to be informed and heard - notice the hearing properly.
- Violating a no-contact or other condition; a warrant may issue immediately (Rule 33.06(b)).

- Filing a bare motion without requesting a hearing date and serving the prosecutor.
- Forgetting the Rule 33.01(d) written 120-day trial request when ordered detained; it must be filed after arraignment.
- Agreeing to a defense continuance after making the 120-day request - that waives it.
- Treating a denial as appealable in the ordinary way; review is by writ.
- Overlooking cost waivers for electronic monitoring and testing for an indigent defendant (Rule 33.01(c)(4), (11)).
- Asking for release to an address that conflicts with a no-contact condition.

## Verify before relying
- Pull current Rule 33.01, 33.05, 33.06, 33.07 and RSMo 544.455 text via `statute-lookup` or Descrybe `search_laws_and_rules` (Rule 33 was rewritten effective 2019-2020).
- Check any case cited on bail for subsequent treatment (CourtListener / Descrybe) before relying on it.
- Recompute the 7-day and 120-day periods from actual dates.
- Legal information, not legal advice; request appointed counsel if jail is possible.
